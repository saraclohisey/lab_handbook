A conference is most useful when you treat it as a chance to make a few meaningful connections,sharpen your thinking and get exposed to new areas of research, not to attend everything.

## Before

- Pick 2–3 goals: e.g., get feedback on a project, meet potential collaborators, or learn a method.

- Build a light schedule around those goals. Leave gaps for conversations, meals, and rest.

- Prepare a one-sentence description of your work and a couple of questions you’d genuinely like to ask.

## During

- Choose sessions selectively; don’t feel obliged to stay if one isn’t useful.

- Ask speakers a focused question, or approach them afterward with a brief introduction.

- Make networking specific and low-pressure: “I’m working on X and was interested in your point about Y. Could I ask you a question?”

- Keep short notes: key ideas, people to contact, and any actions to take. Ask before taking photos of slides.

- Keep an 'ideas' section of your notes, jot down any random ideas taht come to mind, regardless of feasibility. 

- Take breaks. Being alert for a few good conversations beats being exhausted at every event.


## After

- If you’ve made any contacts, follow up within a few days with a short, personal email or message. Mention what you discussed and suggest a clear next step, if appropriate.

- Review your notes and turn the most useful ideas into actions: revise a paper, try a method, or set up a meeting.

- A good measure of success: Did you learn something useful, get helpful feedback, or make one connection worth continuing?

## Time

- Conferences vary in length. The shorter they are, the more focused you’re likely to be on the sessions.

- Conferences lasting two or more days can be exhausting. Take breaks, skip a session if you need to, want to, or feel that resting would help you stay fresh for the more relevant sessions.

## Destination

- You may be somewhere fun and new so make sure you take an opportunity to explore.

- Try to plan at least one excursion, this may be included as part of teh conference but if not try to get to one yourself. Sometimes you might need to miss a session but that's ok.
